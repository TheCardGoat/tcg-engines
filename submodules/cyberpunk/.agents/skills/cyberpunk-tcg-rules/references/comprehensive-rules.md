## 1 — GAME OVERVIEW

## Official Rules Document

### 1.1 — This is an exhaustive document detailing the comprehensive Official Rules for Cyberpunk Trading Card Game (TCG).

### 1.2 — This is a reference material, not a teaching one!

This is a reference material, not a teaching one! To learn how to play the game, check out the {[Gameplay Guide >](https://cyberpunktcg.com/gameplay-guide)}.

### 1.3 — Cyberpunk TCG cards are published in multiple languages.

*Cyberpunk TCG* cards are published in multiple languages. English cards, rules, and clarifications are the official versions, and supersede any conflicting texts in other languages.

## Defining the Game

### 1.4 — A game of Cyberpunk TCG has two (2) players.

A game of *Cyberpunk TCG* has two (2) players. Each player is the other’s opponent, or “Rival.”

#### 1.4.1 — The Official Rules are in second-person for easy reading.

The Official Rules are in second-person for easy reading. Rules addressed to “you” apply to all players in a *Cyberpunk TCG* game.

### 1.5 — The game ends when one player wins the game.

The game ends when one player wins the game. When you win, your Rival loses.

### 1.6 — The game ends when one player loses the game.

The game ends when one player loses the game. When you lose, your Rival wins.

### 1.7 — Every game piece has an owner.

Every game piece has an owner. Every game piece in play also has a controller.

#### 1.7.1 — You “own” the pieces you bring to the game (your deck, side deck, Gig dice, etc.).

You “own” the pieces you bring to the game (your deck, side deck, Gig dice, etc.). You’re the “owner” of these pieces.

#### 1.7.2 — You “control” the pieces currently in your friendly Gig area, field, Legends area, and Eddies area.

You “control” the pieces currently in your friendly Gig area, field, Legends area, and Eddies area. You’re the “controller” of these pieces, even if you’re not the owner.

##### 1.7.2.1 — Pieces you control are considered “friendly” pieces.

##### 1.7.2.2 — Pieces your Rival controls are considered “rival” pieces.

#### 1.7.3 — Game pieces can change controllers, but can never change owners.

##### 1.7.3.1 — When you take control of a Rival’s game piece, follow the rules in {GOLDEN RULES > Respect Your Rival}.

When you take control of a Rival’s game piece, follow the rules in {[GOLDEN RULES > Respect Your Rival](#rule-52e7f11d-d6d2-588d-97dc-609e843c133b)}.

##### 1.7.3.2 — At the end of the game, return all game pieces to their respective owners.

### 1.8 — The game constantly checks its own state.

The game constantly checks its own state. It automatically progresses and/or ends the game accordingly. (Win/lose conditions, turn change, etc.)

### 1.9 — Rules can interrupt to change the game state at any time, even in the middle of effects.

Rules can interrupt to change the game state at any time, even in the middle of effects. They apply automatically as they are relevant, before any effects, actions, or other processes.

## Win Conditions

### 1.10 — If you have seven (7) or more Gig dice in the friendly Gig area at the start of your turn, you win the game.

#### 1.10.1 — The game checks this win condition before the first step of your Start Phase (the Ready Step).

### 1.11 — In overtime, if you have seven (7) or more Gig dice in the friendly Gig area at any point, you immediately win the game.

#### 1.11.1 — Overtime begins after two consecutive turns in which both players begin with an empty fixer area.

Overtime begins after two consecutive turns in which both players begin with an empty fixer area. (Usually, after both players have completed 7 turns each.)

#### 1.11.2 — If you already have seven (7) or more Gig dice in the friendly Gig area when overtime begins, you win immediately.

### 1.12 — If there’s a card effect that specifies an alternate win condition, you win immediately when you meet that condition.

### 1.13 — Winning the game occurs instantly, even in the middle of a game action or effect resolution.

#### 1.13.1 — If both players win simultaneously, the game is considered a tie.

## Lose Conditions

### 1.14 — When you must draw a card from an empty deck (i.e.

When you must draw a card from an empty deck (i.e. there are 0 cards in your deck area), you lose immediately.

#### 1.14.1 — If you attempt to draw more cards than remain in your deck, the first card you cannot draw counts as drawing from an…

If you attempt to draw more cards than remain in your deck, the first card you cannot draw counts as drawing from an empty deck. See {[10.2.1 TERMS > Game Actions > Draw](#rule-b07d7e94-c69d-5162-a49c-f77241f8d44b)}.

> **EXAMPLE:** A card effect says “Draw 2,” but you only have 1 card left in your deck area. You draw the first card, and attempt to draw the second card. The deck is now empty, so you lose immediately.

### 1.15 — If there’s a card effect that specifies an alternate lose condition, you lose if you meet that condition.

### 1.16 — At any point, you may concede.

At any point, you may concede. When you concede, you lose immediately.

#### 1.16.1 — To concede during any point of the game you may tell your Rival they are the winner of the game.

### 1.17 — Losing the game occurs instantly, even in the middle of a game action or effect resolution.

#### 1.17.1 — If both players lose simultaneously, the game is considered a tie.

## 2 — GOLDEN RULES

## Resolve as Much as Possible

### 2.1 — When an effect tells you to do something, you must resolve it.

When an effect tells you to do something, you must resolve it. See {[EFFECTS](#rule-3efdf554-b6f9-5c20-837e-5146363dd5ed)}.

#### 2.1.1 — If an effect includes “you may,” you may choose not to resolve that part of the effect.

### 2.2 — When you activate an effect, you must resolve it.

### 2.3 — Resolve card effects in the order they appear on the card.

### 2.4 — If you can’t resolve the entirety of an effect, resolve as much of it as you possibly can.

## Cards Can Break Rules

### 2.5 — If a card’s text contradicts the Official Rules, the card text supersedes the Official Rules.

If a card’s text contradicts the Official Rules, the card text supersedes the Official Rules.

> **EXAMPLE:** According to the rules, you can’t attack a ready Unit, but a friendly Unit’s card text reads “This Unit can attack ready Units.” The card text overrides that rule, so the friendly Unit can declare an attack on a rival ready Unit.

## “Can’t” Supersedes “Can”

### 2.6 — If an effect on a card allows or requires you to do something but another effect prevents it, the preventing effect a…

If an effect on a card allows or requires you to do something but another effect prevents it, the preventing effect always takes precedence.

> **EXAMPLE:** A Rival plays a Unit with [ADRENALINE], which allows it to attack the turn it’s played. But a friendly Unit’s card text reads “Rival Units can't attack the turn they're played.” The preventing of the attack takes precedence, so the rival Unit can’t attack this turn.

## Players Activate with Discretion

### 2.7 — If an effect has a cost, you may choose not to pay it and thus not activate the effect.

### 2.8 — When cards or effects specify “up to,” you can choose none or 0 (unless there’s a specified minimum).

### 2.9 — If a game mechanic makes both players do an effect at the same time.

If a game mechanic makes both players do an effect at the same time. The turn player will resolve first, followed by their Rival.

## Negative Numbers

### 2.10 — A game value can be a negative number.

#### 2.10.1 — When a game value is a negative number, it is treated as 0 for all references and comparisons of values.

When a game value is a negative number, it is treated as 0 for all references and comparisons of values.

> **EXAMPLE:** A Unit has 2 power. An effect reduces its power by 3. The Unit has -1 power, but is treated as having 0 power when attacking, fighting, or when a card effect references its power. If it then gains +2 power, that Unit has 1 power. The order of these effects being applied does not alter this outcome.

#### 2.10.2 — A value of null ( - ) is not treated as a number.

A value of null ( - ) is not treated as a number. It does not count as 0, and it is not even or odd.

## Respect Your Rival

### 2.11 — Speak to your Rival with civility; no name-calling, yelling, discriminatory acts, intentional misgendering, or other…

Speak to your Rival with civility; no name-calling, yelling, discriminatory acts, intentional misgendering, or other insulting speech.

### 2.12 — Respect your Rival’s personal space and possessions.

#### 2.12.1 — When you take control or wish to review a game piece your Rival owns, ask permission before taking the game piece or…

When you take control or wish to review a game piece your Rival owns, ask permission before taking the game piece or ask the Rival to move their game piece to the proper area.

#### 2.12.2 — Provide reasonable consideration of game pieces owned by Rivals, including handling their game pieces in a respectful…

Provide reasonable consideration of game pieces owned by Rivals, including handling their game pieces in a respectful manner and with permission as necessary.

#### 2.12.3 — Do not touch your Rival without their express permission.

### 2.13 — Come to the table with proper hygiene.

#### 2.13.1 — Keep your hands and body clean.

Keep your hands and body clean. Neutralize any distracting smells on your person, including intense body odor or overwhelming perfumes.

#### 3.20.3 — RAM Limits

#### RAM Limits

A deck’s 3 Legends provide your deck’s “RAM Limits.” All other cards in your deck must be within these RAM Limits.

#### 3.20.4 — By default, decks have a RAM Limit of 0 in all colors (0 Red, 0 Green, 0 Blue, and 0 Yellow).

#### 3.20.5 — When you add a Legend to your deck, increase the deck’s RAM Limits by the Legend’s RAM value.

##### 3.20.5.1 — A deck’s RAM Limit in a color is the sum of its Legends’ RAM in that color.

> **EXAMPLE:** A deck with “Goro Takemura: Hands Unclean” (2 Green RAM), “Saburo Arasaka: Stubborn Patriarch” (2 Green RAM), and “Yorinobu Arasaka: Embracing Destruction” (2 Red RAM), has a RAM Limit of (2 Red, 4 Green, 0 Blue, and 0 Yellow).

##### 3.20.5.2 — Your deck may include any card with RAM less than or equal to your deck’s RAM Limit for its color.

#### 3.20.6 — Your deck’s RAM Limits are locked after deck composition.

Your deck’s RAM Limits are locked after deck composition. If a Legend is removed from the game during play, your deck’s RAM Limits are not affected.

##### 3.20.6.1 — RAM values do not dictate ability to play a card, simply whether it’s legal to include in your deck.

## Gears

### 4.9 — Gear cards can be equipped to your Units and Legends to grant them additional power and/or effects.

### 4.10 — To play a Gear, reveal the card to your Rival, pay its cost, and equip it to a friendly Unit or friendly face-up Legend.

To play a Gear, reveal the card to your Rival, pay its cost, and equip it to a friendly Unit or friendly face-up Legend. See {[TERMS > Game Actions > Equip](#rule-b076fca2-f099-5c4d-b80a-d789a90e2ce9)}.

#### 4.10.1 — By default, a Gear can be equipped to a friendly Unit or friendly face-up Legend.

#### 4.10.2 — You can only equip Gear to a card in the field or Legends area.

#### 4.10.3 — There’s no upper limit on the number of Gears equipped to a single Unit.

### 4.11 — Gears have two textboxes, referred to as the “top textbox” and “bottom textbox.”

#### 4.11.1 — The top textbox contains effects for the Gear.

The top textbox contains effects for the Gear. These effects are relevant when you play the Gear, and generally not relevant after a Gear is equipped.

#### 4.11.2 — Effects described in the top textbox apply to the Gear itself, and are not inherited by the Unit or Legend it’s equip…

Effects described in the top textbox apply to the Gear itself, and are not inherited by the Unit or Legend it’s equipped to.

> **EXAMPLE:** A Gear’s top text box contains the reminder text “*(Equip to a friendly Unit or face-up Legend.)*” You equip this Gear to a friendly Unit. That Gear cannot be equipped to another friendly Unit, nor face-up Legend.

#### 4.11.3 — The bottom textbox contains effects inherited by the Unit or Legend the Gear is equipped to.

The bottom textbox contains effects inherited by the Unit or Legend the Gear is equipped to. Read the bottom textbox as if it's printed on that Unit or Legend.

##### 4.11.3.1 — Effects described in a Gear card’s bottom textbox apply only to the Unit or Legend the Gear is equipped to, not the G…

Effects described in a Gear card’s bottom textbox apply only to the Unit or Legend the Gear is equipped to, not the Gear itself.

##### 4.11.3.2 — A Unit or Legend does not inherit Tags or other characteristics from a Gear equipped to it.

### 4.12 — When a Legend or Unit moves to a different area (field, trash, hand, etc.), all of its equipped Gear moves with it.

#### 4.12.1 — When a Gear moves with a Unit or Legend to an area outside of the field or Legends area, the Gear isn’t equipped to t…

When a Gear moves with a Unit or Legend to an area outside of the field or Legends area, the Gear isn’t equipped to the Unit or Legend in that area.

> **EXAMPLE:** An equipped Unit has the effect “At the end of your turn, return this Unit to its owner's hand.” When you move the Unit to your hand, you also move its equipped Gear to your hand. Once the cards are in your hand, the Gear is no longer equipped to the Unit. So, if you play the Unit from your hand next turn, the Gear does not move with it.

#### 4.12.2 — If an equipped Legend moves to an area outside of the Field or Legends area, the Gear follows the Legend to the new a…

If an equipped Legend moves to an area outside of the Field or Legends area, the Gear follows the Legend to the new area. The Gear is no longer equipped in the new area, so it stays there when you remove the Legend from play.

#### 4.12.3 — When you bottom-deck an equipped Unit, place the cards at the bottom of your deck in a random order.

When you bottom-deck an equipped Unit, place the cards at the bottom of your deck in a random order. Randomize them amongst any other cards bottom-decked at the same time. See {[TERMS > Game Actions > Bottom-deck](#rule-3cb91d69-87a6-5c59-a0ea-7aae60987910)}.

## Programs

### 4.13 — Programs provide immediate one-time effects.

### 4.14 — To play a Program, reveal the card to your Rival, pay its cost, resolve its effect(s), and immediately move it to you…

To play a Program, reveal the card to your Rival, pay its cost, resolve its effect(s), and immediately move it to your trash.

#### 4.14.1 — When you reveal a played Program, place it on the table to allow your Rival to read and understand its effects before…

When you reveal a played Program, place it on the table to allow your Rival to read and understand its effects before you resolve it.

After you pay a Program’s cost, the card is considered outside of your hand, field, trash, or any other game area until you finish resolving its effects (though it’s not considered “Removed from Play”). After you resolve the program, it enters your trash.

## 6 — GIGS

## 6.1 — 1 polyhedral die represents 1 Gig.

1 polyhedral die represents 1 Gig. Gigs are also referred to as “Gig dice” (and variations thereof).

### 6.1.1 — Gig dice are represented by d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20.

Gig dice are represented by d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20. These designations refer both to the number of sides on a die and the highest value a die can reach.

### 6.1.2 — You control the Gigs in your Gig area.

You control the Gigs in your Gig area. They are considered “friendly Gigs”.

### 6.1.3 — You don’t control the Gig dice in your fixer area.

You don’t control the Gig dice in your fixer area. They are not considered friendly Gigs.

### 6.1.4 — You can’t target Gig dice in the fixer area to fulfill or resolve effects.

## 6.2 — Whenever you roll a die, you may use any method you want as long as it is timely and meets these requirements:

### 6.2.1 — Your roll method properly randomizes the die faces.

### 6.2.2 — The result is immediately visible when the die stops rolling.

### 6.2.3 — No one touches the rolling die or interferes with its trajectory until it is completely still and shows a definitive…

No one touches the rolling die or interferes with its trajectory until it is completely still and shows a definitive result.

### 6.2.4 — The die lands on a definitive result, not cocked between faces.

### 6.2.5 — If your roll breaks any of the above rules, ignore the result and roll it again properly.

### 6.2.6 — When you move a die to the area of play after rolling, you must preserve the value.

## 6.3 — A Gig’s value is the number on its current top face.

### 6.3.1 — A min Gig is any Gig die at its lowest possible value.

### 6.3.2 — A max Gig is any Gig die at its highest possible value.

A max Gig is any Gig die at its highest possible value.

> **EXAMPLE:** A d4 has four sides with possible values of 1, 2, 3, and 4. If its current value is 4, then it’s a max Gig.

### 6.3.3 — The highest value on a die is always equal to its number of sides, and the lowest value on a die is always 1.

The highest value on a die is always equal to its number of sides, and the lowest value on a die is always 1. This is true even if the specific die represents the number differently.

> **EXAMPLE:** If the faces of your d10 are labelled 0-9, the 0 represents “10” and is considered the highest value, while the 1 is considered the lowest value.

## 6.4 — Adjust a Gig by changing its value.

### 6.4.1 — “Increase” a Gig by adjusting to a higher number.

### 6.4.2 — “Decrease” a Gig by adjusting to a lower number.

### 6.4.3 — “Set” a Gig by adjusting it to a specific value.

### 6.4.4 — You cannot adjust a Gig to a value that isn’t on its face.

You cannot adjust a Gig to a value that isn’t on its face. (i.e. set a d6 to 8). If an effect tells you to do so, the effect fails.

### 6.4.5 — A Gig’s value cannot be adjusted to the value it already has.

A Gig’s value cannot be adjusted to the value it already has. If an effect tells you to do so, the effect fails.

> **EXAMPLE:** If a Gig is at a min value, it cannot be changed to its min value by an effect. You may choose it for an effect that could adjust it to a min, but nothing would happen.

## 6.5 — A value-pair is 2 dice with the same value in the same Gig area.

### 6.5.1 — A Gig can only count towards a single value-pair at a time.

A Gig can only count towards a single value-pair at a time.

> **EXAMPLE:** A Unit’s card text reads “Draw 1 for each friendly value-pair of Gigs.” And your Gig area contains three Gig dice with the same value: a d4, d6, and d8. The d4 and d6 may be counted as a value-pair, and then there are no more Gigs to pair with the d8. So, there is exactly one friendly value-pair of Gigs. Draw 1 card.

To swap a Gig is to choose a friendly Gig die and a rival Gig die, then move the friendly Gig die to the rival Gig area and the rival Gig die to the friendly Gig area at the same time.

### 6.6.1 — The values on both Gigs remain the same throughout the swap, and cannot be adjusted during it.

### 6.6.2 — A swap is not considered stealing, and doesn’t trigger any effects related to stealing.

## 6.7 — To steal a Gig is to take control of a rival Gig, such that it becomes a friendly Gig.

### 6.7.1 — Whenever a Gig is stolen, there is an “actor” that steals it.

#### 6.7.1.1 — When a Unit/Legend attacks to steal a Gig, they are the actor, the one doing the stealing

#### 6.7.2.1 — Choose the rival Gig(s) to steal.

#### 6.7.2.2 — Move the Gig(s) to your Gig area.

##### 6.7.2.2.1 — When an actor steals multiple Gigs at the same, choose both at the same time, then move both at the same time.

#### 6.7.2.3 — You take control of the Gig(s).

You take control of the Gig(s). They become friendly Gigs.

### 6.7.3 — If a card effect triggers when a Gig is stolen, resolve the effect after the Gig changes control.

If a card effect triggers when a Gig would be stolen, resolve the effect after the Gig is selected, but before the Gig changes control.

> **EXAMPLE:** A Unit’s card text reads “When a rival Unit would steal a Gig, you may discard 1 with cost equal to that Gig's value. If you do, the Gig isn't stolen.”

A Gig’s value can’t change during stealing. If an effect adjusts the Gig’s value, you must resolve it either before choosing the Gig(s), or after the Gig(s) change control.

## 9 — ATTACK, FIGHT, AND STEAL

## 9.1 — “Attack” is a game action in which you spend a friendly ready Unit to attack a valid target, which results in the fri…

“Attack” is a game action in which you spend a friendly ready Unit to attack a valid target, which results in the friendly Unit fighting or stealing.

## 9.2 — You may attack as a Main Phase action.

You may attack as a Main Phase action. See {[TURN ORDER > Main Phase](#rule-6b35d117-68b9-5655-992b-d642e8de476d)}.

## Declare the Attack

### 9.3 — To attack, you must declare the attack by completing these steps in order.

#### 9.3.1 — Choose a friendly ready Unit in the field area that can attack.

##### 9.3.1.1 — Lagging Units can’t attack.

Lagging Units can’t attack. See {TERMS > General > Lag}.

#### 9.3.2 — Choose a valid attack target for that Unit.

##### 9.3.2.1 — A spent rival Unit is a valid attack target.

##### 9.3.2.2 — A rival Gig area containing one or more Gigs is a valid attack target.

A rival Gig area containing one or more Gigs is a valid attack target. (An empty Gig area is not a valid attack target.)

##### 9.3.2.3 — Card effects may add to, remove, or restrict a Unit’s valid attack targets.

###### 9.3.2.3.1 — An effect that allows a Unit to attack only grants the specific permission described by that effect and does not remo…

An effect that allows a Unit to attack only grants the specific permission described by that effect and does not remove other attack restrictions.

##### 9.3.2.4 — If there are no valid targets, you cannot attack.

#### 9.3.3 — Spend the friendly Unit.

#### 9.3.4 — Communicate the attack and the attack target to your Rival.

### 9.4 — When you declare an attack to your Rival, the game process of “attacking” begins immediately.

#### 9.4.1 — While attacking, you are the “attacking player.”

#### 9.4.2 — While you’re attacking, your Rival is the “defending player.”

#### 9.4.3 — The Unit you spend to attack is the “attacker” or “attacking Unit.” It attacks the selected target.

#### 9.4.4 — If your attack target is a Unit, it’s called the “defending Unit,” “defender,” or “opposing Unit.”

#### 9.4.5 — If your attack target is the rival Gig area, it’s called the “defending Gig area.”

### 9.5 — When you declare the attack to your Rival, apply effects that last “while attacking” and variations thereof.

When you declare the attack to your Rival, apply effects that last “while attacking” and variations thereof. All effects triggered by attacking or spending the attacker become pending. See {[EFFECTS > Pending Resolution](#rule-9afed67a-613b-547b-bbc7-75c55ca75d57)}.

### 9.6 — After all pending effects are resolved, if the attacker or the defending Unit is no longer in the field area, the att…

After all pending effects are resolved, if the attacker or the defending Unit is no longer in the field area, the attack ends. See {[Ending an Attack](#rule-ea5b950d-f8e1-5b3d-8cd5-20dec6ef700a)}. Otherwise, the attack continues; proceed to the React Step.

## React Step

### 9.7 — During the “React Step” of an attack, the defending player has an opportunity to react.

During the “React Step” of an attack, the defending player has an opportunity to react. They may take as many reactions as they want, in any order they choose.

#### 9.7.1 — You may only take reactions during the React Step of an attack, and only if you are the defending player.

### 9.8 — You may Call a Legend as a reaction.

You may Call a Legend as a reaction. See {[TERMS > Game Actions > Call a Legend](#rule-5a78278b-9f8b-5e64-b9aa-b2936f705af9)}.

### 9.9 — You may activate the [BLOCKER] effect on a ready Unit as a reaction.

#### 9.9.1 — When a Unit uses [BLOCKER], it becomes the defending Unit, fully replacing the previous attack target.

### 9.10 — You may activate any effects with the [QUICK] keyword as a reaction.

You may activate any effects with the [QUICK] keyword as a reaction. See {[TERMS > Effect Keywords > QUICK](#rule-86f65009-eda8-56d8-bc0d-58faa877bea9)}.

#### 9.10.1 — If an effect on a Program in your hand has the [QUICK] keyword, you may play that Program as a reaction.

### 9.11 — If a reaction triggers one or more effects, those effects become pending.

If a reaction triggers one or more effects, those effects become pending. Both players must resolve all pending effects before the defending player may take another reaction. See {[ACTIVATE & RESOLVE EFFECTS > Triggered Effects](#rule-9afed67a-613b-547b-bbc7-75c55ca75d57)}.

### 9.12 — At any point during the React Step, while not taking a reaction and while there are no effects pending resolution, th…

At any point during the React Step, while not taking a reaction and while there are no effects pending resolution, the defending player may choose to end the React Step.

### 9.13 — If at any point the attacker or the defending Unit is no longer in the field area, the attack ends.

If at any point the attacker or the defending Unit is no longer in the field area, the attack ends. See {[Ending an Attack](#rule-ea5b950d-f8e1-5b3d-8cd5-20dec6ef700a)}.

### 9.14 — When the React Step ends, the attack continues.

When the React Step ends, the attack continues. If the attack target is a Unit, proceed to the Fight Step. If the attack target is a Gig area, proceed to the Steal Step.

## Fight Step

### 9.15 — The Fight Step only happens if the attack target is a Unit.

The Fight Step only happens if the attack target is a Unit. If the attack target is a Gig Area, the Steal Step happens instead.

### 9.16 — During the “Fight Step” of an attack, the attacking Unit and the defending Unit fight.

#### 9.16.1 — At the start of the Fight Step, apply effects that last “while fighting” and variations thereof.

### 9.17 — A “fight” is a game process in which two opposing Units compare power to determine at least one loser and potentially…

A “fight” is a game process in which two opposing Units compare power to determine at least one loser and potentially one winner.

#### 9.17.1 — A Unit wins a fight when it has a higher power than the opposing Unit.

##### 9.17.1.1 — When a Unit wins a fight, the opposing Unit always loses, regardless of power.

#### 9.17.2 — A Unit loses a fight when it has equal or lower power than the opposing Unit.

#### 9.17.3 — If both Units in a fight have the same power, neither Unit wins and both Units lose the fight.

#### 9.17.4 — If a card effect says that a Unit wins or loses a fight, it supersedes any win or loss status determined by power com…

If a card effect says that a Unit wins or loses a fight, it supersedes any win or loss status determined by power comparison.

### 9.18 — When you determine the loser(s), any effects triggered by the fight (i.e.

When you determine the loser(s), any effects triggered by the fight (i.e. “when this Unit fights,” “when this Unit loses a fight,” etc.) become pending. Both players must resolve all pending effects before they may defeat the loser(s).

### 9.19 — After resolving all effects triggered by the fight, resolve the fight’s result.

#### 9.19.1 — When a Unit loses a fight, the opposing Unit defeats it as a result.

##### 9.19.1.1 — When a Unit is defeated, its owner moves it to their trash.

#### 9.19.2 — A Unit with 0 power cannot defeat a Unit as the result of a fight.

#### 9.19.3 — If an effect prevents a Unit from being defeated in a fight, it is still a loser that has lost the fight, it only ign…

If an effect prevents a Unit from being defeated in a fight, it is still a loser that has lost the fight, it only ignores the resulting defeat.

### 9.20 — After resolving all effects triggered by the fight’s result, the Fight Step and the attack ends.

After resolving all effects triggered by the fight’s result, the Fight Step and the attack ends. See {[Ending an Attack](#rule-ea5b950d-f8e1-5b3d-8cd5-20dec6ef700a)}.

## Steal Step

### 9.21 — The Steal Step only happens if the attack target is a Gig area.

The Steal Step only happens if the attack target is a Gig area. If the attack target is a Unit, the Fight Step happens instead.

### 9.22 — If the attacked Gig area has no Gigs in it when the Steal Step begins, no Gigs can be or are stolen.

### 9.23 — During the “Steal Step” of an attack, the attacker attempts to steal one or more Gig dice from the defending Gig area.

#### 9.23.1 — At the start of the Steal Step, before choosing which Gigs are stolen, apply effects that last “while stealing” and v…

At the start of the Steal Step, before choosing which Gigs are stolen, apply effects that last “while stealing” and variations thereof. Apply any other effects that modify, prevent, or restrict the attacker’s power or ability to steal.

#### 9.23.2 — The number of Gigs an attacker steals is based on its power.

##### 9.23.2.1 — If the attacking Unit has 0 power, it steals 0 Gigs.

##### 9.23.2.2 — If the attacking Unit has 1 or more power, it steals X Gigs (rounded down) according to this formula, where P = the a…

If the attacking Unit has 1 or more power, it steals X Gigs (rounded down) according to this formula, where P = the attacking Unit’s power.

X = (P / 10) + 1)

> **EXAMPLE:** A Unit with 1 to 9 power steals 1 Gig. A Unit with 10 to 19 power steals 2 Gigs. Continue this pattern for higher power values.

#### 9.23.3 — The attacking player chooses which Gigs the attacker will steal from the attacked Gig area.

##### 9.23.3.1 — An attacking player may choose to steal any Gig in the defending Gig area, regardless of its value.

##### 9.23.3.2 — If the attacker will steal multiple Gigs, the attacking player chooses the Gigs one at a time.

##### 9.23.3.3 — If the attacker steals more Gigs than are in the attacked Gig area, choose as many Gigs as possible to steal.

#### 9.23.4 — After all Gigs are chosen, apply any effects that prevent or restrict one or more of those Gigs from being stolen.

#### 9.23.5 — After all Gigs are chosen and prevention effects are applied, the attacker steals the Gig(s).

##### 9.23.5.1 — To steal the Gig(s), move all chosen Gigs from the defending Gig area to the attacking Gig area at the same time.

##### 9.23.5.2 — Multiple Gigs are stolen at the same time, each stolen Gig is considered a separate stolen Gig.

#### 9.23.6 — Effects that trigger when a Gig is stolen become pending after a stolen Gig is moved.

### 9.24 — Resolve all pending effects.

Resolve all pending effects. See {[EFFECTS > Pending Resolution](#rule-9afed67a-613b-547b-bbc7-75c55ca75d57)}.

### 9.25 — After all pending effects from the Steal Step are resolved, the attack ends.

After all pending effects from the Steal Step are resolved, the attack ends. See {[Ending an Attack](#rule-ea5b950d-f8e1-5b3d-8cd5-20dec6ef700a)}.

## Ending an Attack

### 9.26 — An attack ends if any of the following are true:

#### 9.26.1 — The attacking Unit is no longer in the field area.

#### 9.26.2 — The attack target is no longer in the area it was attacked in.

#### 9.26.3 — The attack target is no longer a legal attack target for the attacking Unit.

#### 9.26.4 — An effect has ended the attack.

### 9.27 — An attack can end before reaching the React Step, Fight Step or Steal Step.

#### 9.27.1 — If an attack ends before reaching the React Step, Fight Step or Steal Step, no fight happens and no Gigs are stolen.

#### 9.27.2 — If an attack ends while an effect or pending effect is resolving, finish resolving that effect or pending effect.

### 9.28 — When an attack ends, resolve any pending effects that are already pending for the current timing window.

### 9.29 — After an attack ends and all effects and pending effects for the current timing window have resolved, remove effects…

After an attack ends and all effects and pending effects for the current timing window have resolved, remove effects that last “while attacking,” “while stealing,” “while fighting” and variations thereof. The turn player’s Main Phase continues.

If you control the source of an effect, it is considered “your” effect. You are the one who resolves it.

### 10.2.5 — After an effect resolves, perform any required rule processing before resolving a new one or taking any actions.

#### 10.6.2 — If part of an effect is invalid, ignore the invalid part and resolve as much of the remaining effect as possible.

#### 10.6.3 — Even if an effect is invalid, it is still considered an effect on the card.

If an effect requires a target or choice with no legal options, the effect is invalid and the controlling player does not make a choice.

## Pending Resolution

### 10.11 — A “pending” effect is an effect that is waiting to resolve.

#### 10.11.3 — After a pending effect resolves, it stops being pending.

If both players control pending effects at the same time, the turn player resolves all their pending effects before their Rival resolves all of theirs.

If the same trigger condition is met multiple times, the triggered effect becomes pending once for each time that condition is met.

### 11.13 — Search

### Search

#### 11.13.1 — “Search” is a game action in which you look at a designated amount of cards.

##### 11.13.1.1 — When an effect says “Search X” (where X is a number), look at X number of cards from the top of your deck.

#### 11.13.2 — Once you fulfill any “Search” requirements, if the card does not tell you where to place the cards afterward, return…

Once you fulfill any “Search” requirements, if the card does not tell you where to place the cards afterward, return the cards to their original location in the same order.

> **EXAMPLE:** A Legend’s effect text reads “When a friendly equipped Unit is defeated, search the top 2 cards of your deck and trash 1.” You look at the top 2 cards of your deck, move one to the trash, and place the other one back on the top of your deck.

#### 11.13.3 — While searching cards in an area, the cards still count towards the total number of cards in that area.

While searching cards in an area, the cards still count towards the total number of cards in that area. The card does not leave the area until (and unless) you move it to a new game area.

> **EXAMPLE:** An effect requires you to search the top 3 cards of your deck. Your deck currently contains only 2 cards. You search as many cards as you can (2), but you do not immediately lose; because you did not draw them, they’re still in your deck.

#### 11.13.4 — When an effect allows you to search one of your hidden areas for a specific card or criteria, if you fail to find any…

When an effect allows you to search one of your hidden areas for a specific card or criteria, if you fail to find anything, you don’t have to show the hidden information to the Rival player. See {[GAME AREAS > General > Hidden Areas](#rule-3fae03cf-a089-553e-91d3-88e0694e138c)}.

##### 11.13.4.1 — When you search a hidden area for a specific target(s) and you’re not obligated to reveal the searched cards, you may…

When you search a hidden area for a specific target(s) and you’re not obligated to reveal the searched cards, you may purposely fail to find any or all target(s).

> **EXAMPLE:** An effect says “Search the top 3 cards of your deck. Reveal a Gear and add it to your hand. Bottom-deck the rest.” When you search the top 3 cards of your deck, you find 2 Gear cards. But, because your deck is a hidden area, you may choose not to reveal any of them, and bottom-deck all 3 cards.

## Activation Shorthand

### 11.15 — [Spend Icon] (Spend Icon)

### [Spend Icon] (Spend Icon)

#### 11.15.1 — The [Spend Icon] symbol is shorthand for an activation cost.

The [Spend Icon] symbol is shorthand for an activation cost. It means: “Spend this Unit or Legend to activate the following effect.”

> **EXAMPLE:** A friendly Unit has the effect “[Spend Icon] Draw 1.” The [Spend Icon] symbol is an activation trigger. You must specifically spend this Unit as a cost. If you do, you draw a card.

#### 11.15.2 — To activate a [Spend Icon] effect, you must spend the card with the express purpose of paying its activation cost.

To activate a [Spend Icon] effect, you must spend the card with the express purpose of paying its activation cost. When you spend a Unit or Legend with this symbol for any other reason, it does not trigger the [Spend Icon] effect.

#### 11.15.3 — After paying the cost to use a [Spend Icon] effect, immediately resolve the effect.

##### 11.15.3.1 — If a [Spend Icon] effect also has a condition, the condition must be met at the time of activation and resolution.

##### 11.15.3.2 — If additional effects conditions are met in the process of using a [Spend Icon:] effect, resolve the [Spend Icon] eff…

If additional effects conditions are met in the process of using a [Spend Icon:] effect, resolve the [Spend Icon] effect, then resolve any other pending effects.

### 11.16 — X €$

### X €$

#### 11.16.1 — “X €$” (where X is a number) denotes Eddies and/or Legends spent to pay a cost.

##### 11.16.1.1 — When bolded and preceding a colon, before the effect text, it represents an activation cost (or part of an activation…

When bolded and preceding a colon, before the effect text, it represents an activation cost (or part of an activation cost) for the effect. It means: “Pay X to activate the following effect.”

> **EXAMPLE:** A friendly Legend has the text “1 €$, [Spend Icon] Give a friendly Unit +1 power this turn for each of its equipped Gear.“ So, to activate this effect, you must spend this Legend and 1 other Eddie or Legend.
