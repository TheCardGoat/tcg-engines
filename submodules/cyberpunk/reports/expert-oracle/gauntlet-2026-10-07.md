# Cyberpunk Bot Lab gauntlet — 2026-10-07

## Method

The run ranks 15 authored decks with Sharp in both seats. It then tests all 14
distinct strategy implementations with identical decks for both players, using
the top three decks. The `default` alias is counted once as Expert.

| Phase | Games | Seeds | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Deck selection | 420 | 2 per deck pair, both seats | Rank the 15 authored decks |
| Strategy screen | 1,092 | 2 per deck, every strategy pair, both seats | Compare all 14 strategies |
| Finalists | 90 | 5 fresh seeds per deck, every finalist pair, both seats | Check the top three competitive strategies |
| Choombattler reference | 12 | 2 per deck, both seats | Compare adapted Expert with the original Expert worker |

The screen and finalist phases reuse strategy-independent shuffle seeds across
opponents. Strategies retain their shipped information policies: Expert uses
hidden information; the other native strategies use public information. Monte
Carlo uses the AI runner defaults of one rollout per action and ten rollout
steps. MCTS uses its shipped defaults of 50 iterations and 200 rollout steps.
Native strategies use the existing authored-deck profile binding. No weights
were tuned during this gauntlet.

The six controls are first-legal, random, attack-rival-only, attack-unit-only,
call-legend-only, and pass-only. They are included in the screen but excluded
from finalist selection and the competitive score. An illegal action, stuck
choice, action cap, or forced automation concession is an invalid game, not a
normal opponent win. A strategy with its own failure in a competitive game
cannot become a finalist. All screen failures are also reported separately.

## Gig adjustment repair

An effect that says “up to” can resolve with a zero adjustment amount by
selecting the Gig's current value. The engine pays the required cost and resumes
the effect, but does not emit `gigValueChanged` or activate a payoff that requires
the value to change. An exact adjustment still rejects an unchanged value. Bots
now exclude unchanged values when planning exact adjustments.

Setting a Gig to another Gig's value is a separate effect. Peace Offering already
accepts two Gigs with the same value: the set does nothing and resolution
continues, including its separate check for a value-pair. This behavior is covered
by its real-card tests.

A zero adjustment amount does not mean a Gig face value of zero. Valid faces
remain 1 through the die maximum; a printed 0 on a d10 means 10.

This follows [Comprehensive Rules 2.8, 6.3.3, and 6.4.4–6.4.5](https://tcg.online/cyberpunk/llm/rules.txt).
The relevant printed example is [Dexter DeShawn — Off the Grid](https://tcg.online/cyberpunk/cards/dexter-deshawn-off-the-grid):
its Spend effect can increase a Gig by up to two.

The engine automation revision is `cyberpunk-automation-v14`. Earlier partial
runs were stopped after replay-confirmed legality failures. Their records are
not mixed into this run.

## Reference boundary

The reference uses the original worker's Expert message handler. Both players
execute the Choombattler native reducer. Our Expert chooser receives the native
legal actions through a projection that omits active effects, granted rules,
and pending combat details, and approximates some turn flags. Both choosers can
use hidden information. This is an adapter diagnostic, separate from the native
strategy ranking; it cannot establish production strength or promote a bot.
