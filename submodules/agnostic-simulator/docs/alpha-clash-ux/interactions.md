## ac-setup — Opening and mulligan

**Intent and context.** Start a seeded native game with validated 50-card decks, eight-card opening hands and empty play zones. Each seat first declares Keep or Replace in native order. Only after both declarations are complete may replacing players submit their private selection. Zero through eight cards are supported. Start Game is a separate native action after both selections finish. `opening-preview` uses the native initializer; no hand, pending choice or move is fabricated.

**Visual sequence.** Available: reveal contenders and deal the owner's faces/opponent backs; retain public counts. Hover/focus: inspect an owned hand card without changing its replacement state. Selected: lift chosen cards 4 px and add a replace-shaped marker plus selected count. Pending: hold selected positions and disable duplicate submission; the waiting seat sees its own hand and a concise waiting message. Resolving: when native replacement resolves, return only selected cards, shuffle, then deal replacements; retained cards keep their order. Completed: expose Start Game only after native readiness, then show turn-one Resource Step. Zero-card replacement has no card flight. [Annotated states](storyboards.html#setup).

**Input behavior.** Click or keyboard-activate Keep/Declare mulligan. Declarations are commitments; do not present Cancel as an engine-supported reversal. In the private selection, click or Space toggles a card; drag into a replacement lane is an equivalent proposed path. Right click or the inspection action opens card detail. Selection changes are local until the count-labelled confirmation is activated. “Replace 0 selected cards” must be deliberate and remains distinct from the earlier Keep declaration. Escape clears an uncommitted selection or closes inspection; it cannot reverse an accepted choice. Tab reaches all eight cards and confirmation without losing focus in the fan. A stale/repeated submit must not redraw again.

**Board communication.** Keep the declaration step distinct from the selection step. Use a replace icon and solid selection frame, not color alone. Show the owner’s selected count beside confirmation. Do not show another player's selected identities or draft markers; reveal counts only when native policy permits. Retain the public starting-player indicator. In hot-seat review, seat switching must update both candidates and status; production remote views keep a fixed seat. Waiting never means that the player should repeat a committed command.

**Motion timeline.** Use the shared 180 ms tray entry and 140 ms selection feedback. Accepted return travels to deck over 320 ms; shuffle uses one 180 ms deck-stack motion; replacement deal uses 320 ms travel with 40 ms stagger, within the shared batch budget. Retained cards do not fly out and back. Source placeholders preserve spatial order until native resolution. Layer travelling cards above the board but below inspection. A rejection starts no replacement choreography. Reset cancels queued beats. A new native prompt is actionable immediately. Reduced motion uses static selection frames, immediate native positions and an 80 ms fade; zero replacement stays stationary.

**Sound cues.** One pick/target tick per selection gesture with shared cooldown; one confirm only after native acceptance. Return/shuffle uses an identity-neutral paper cue, and replacement landing uses place with at most two simultaneous voices. No card-specific audio exposes the opposing hand. Start Game uses one ready cue after native acceptance. Deduplicate all beats by action/state version; master mute suppresses current and queued voices. The visible count, card backs, status and phase communicate the full sequence without audio.

**Acceptance.** Native tests cover both declarations, both seats, one-time replacement, zero/all, private candidates, deterministic reset and turn-one deployment. Browser evidence now covers both players declaring Replace, Player 2 committing all eight, Player 1 committing zero, and Start Game appearing only after both finish. Single-replacement and earlier normal/muted/reduced-motion recordings are separate evidence. Repeat the both-replace boundary with keyboard, all three viewports, audio and reduced motion before claiming full acceptance. Verify original Player 1 faces are retained, Player 2 receives a valid eight-card hand, deck counts remain 42, and a second replacement is unavailable. Historical opening source is proven by native initialization; that does not certify arranged midgame fixtures.

**Current-code checkpoint (2026-10-10).** Confirmation now submits the native mulligan immediately. A first locked selection keeps the exact old hand stationary while the other seat chooses; no return/shuffle/deal runs from an unresolved selection. After the final native resolution, the baseline presents the accepted replacement deal. Animation timers never submit gameplay, and WebGL cancellation does not undo an accepted selection. An outgoing snapshot is still absent, so return/shuffle is not implemented by moving the already-dealt replacement faces backward. Keep that motion gap explicit. The native public mulligan.resolved flag now reaches the local and live board model; only this boolean is copied, with no private selection IDs. The fixture selector is available during static review; selecting another view does not grant its gameplay rights.

## ac-resource — Deploy or skip resource

**Intent and context.** During the active seat's native Resource Step, put one eligible hand card into the Resource Zone or skip. Use `resource-step`: real cards expose ordinary legal choices and native Rivaled/Bound restrictions. The actor sees their hand; the other seat sees only the hand count until a resource is publicly deployed. The phase, candidate set and destination come from the adapter.

**Visual sequence.** Available: keep the hand readable and show one landing outline in the Resource Zone. Hover/focus: put matching corner marks on a legal card and the landing outline; ineligible cards remain inspectable without that mark. Selected: lift the proposed card 4 px and draw a dotted destination line; retain its hand slot. Pending: hold the proposal and disable only duplicate deploy/skip. Resolving: after acceptance move the card to the public resource row and invert it at landing. Completed: show the new ready-resource total, remove the landing outline and expose native Primary actions. Skip removes the outline without card travel. [Six-state layout](storyboards.html#resource).

**Input behavior.** Drag a legal hand card into the zone; click-card then click-zone is equivalent. Tab reaches legal cards, the destination and Skip; Space selects, Enter confirms, Escape clears an uncommitted draft. Outside drop cancels without spending the step. Selecting a different card replaces the draft. Skip is a separate deliberate action; it cannot be triggered by an empty drop. Once either native command is accepted, a second deployment is unavailable. Reject stale or ineligible submissions without moving any card; retain only still-valid selections for retry.

**Board communication.** The zone outline uses a plus-shaped resource symbol and “Add resource” accessible name. Preview the proposed count, such as 1 → 2, without updating the real count before acceptance. Show native ineligibility reasons on card focus. Do not encode ready/spent or eligibility with color alone. The opponent sees the accepted card only when the native projection makes it public; a private hover or draft must not reveal its art, name or cost.

**Motion timeline.** Focus 100 ms; selection 140 ms; accepted transfer 320 ms with the shared shallow arc. Start inversion during the final 100 ms and settle at the row anchor. Layer the travelling card above board cards but below inspection and mandatory choices. The old hand gap closes over 160 ms. A skip uses only the 220 ms phase transition. On resize, update the endpoint; on new mandatory choice, stop decorative tails and focus that choice. Reduced motion puts the card directly in its native zone with an 80 ms fade and a static destination mark.

**Sound cues.** Pick at gesture start, once per gesture, −20 dB. Place at accepted landing, −16 dB, one voice. Skip uses decline at −22 dB after acceptance. Invalid drops are silent; deliberate native rejection uses the shared restrained reject cue. Apply event deduplication, shared voice limits and master mute. The resource count, inversion and phase marker carry the same information without audio.

**Acceptance.** From reset test legal deploy, selection change, cancelled drop, Skip, ineligible Rivaled/Bound input and duplicate/stale submit. Check the final resource count, exact card zone, ready state and Primary phase through the native adapter. Verify actor/opponent privacy before and after commitment. Capture normal, reduced-motion and muted flows at all three sizes; include keyboard and drag paths. Native tests exist in `resource-step-fixture.test.ts`; full browser/audio acceptance remains open. Arranged board history remains separate from construction validation.

**Current board path and commit point.** Card inspection now exposes Deploy as resource only for native eligible candidates. Enter on that button submits immediately through the shared draft; the proposed click-card/destination preview remains future UX. Escape or Close before pressing Deploy closes inspection without spending the step. Hand paging must preserve card identity, including duplicates, and keep the remaining page reachable after deployment.

Before automatic seat handoff, Player 1 saw the opponent count rise 8→9 and deck fall 42→41 without opponent card names. After handoff, Player 2 saw its own faces and Player 1's backs. Next hand cards → Enter Sonoro (position 9 of 9) → Enter Deploy as resource moved hand 9→8, resources 0→1 ready, and Expansion→Primary; deck stayed 41. ## ac-priority — Priority, pass and waiting

**Intent and context.** The seat named by native decision priority can respond or pass exactly one window. Pending choices, obstruction and buff steps can assign a decision to a seat different from the active-turn player. Use `clash-play` and `arena-clash` to observe the response chain from both seats. Public source cards remain visible; hidden hand identities and private choice candidates remain private.

**Visual sequence.** Available: a fixed action control and a small seat-edge marker identify whose decision is due. Hover/focus: Pass explains the current window; a legal response card shows its action affordance. Selected: a response draft retains the pending source and its place in Standby. Pending: a submitted Pass displays a compact pending state once, without moving cards. Resolving: the next native state changes the seat marker or resolves an effect. Completed: show the new decision and keep unresolved source relationships visible. The waiting seat has an inspectable board and a concise “Player 2 responding” status, not an active Pass button. [Annotated layout](storyboards.html#priority).

**Input behavior.** Click or Enter on Pass submits one advertised action with the current request/state version. Space can activate the focused native button. Holding a key must not pass multiple future windows. A response draft supports the shared cancel/reselect path until committed; Pass itself has no speculative undo. Disable duplicate activation while pending, then derive fresh controls from the next view. Do not add auto-pass policy in this stage. A seat without priority cannot reuse another seat's action. Stale requests reject and refresh without silently passing a later window.

**Board communication.** Distinguish active turn from decision priority. Use a stable seat marker, shape and short text; never flash the whole board. A mandatory target/order prompt takes precedence over the generic waiting caption. Keep source art, response order and selected targets inspectable. The public history names the actual actor and action; avoid empty sentences such as “passed” with no player. No timer is implied unless the adapter provides authoritative timing data.

**Motion timeline.** Move emphasis through a 220 ms ease-out opacity transition at the seat edge. Plain Pass causes no card transfer or impact. The newly legal control is usable immediately; the phase fade cannot delay it. Native resolution uses the source interaction's motion sequence and can interrupt the marker fade. Queue no repeated pulse. Reduced motion replaces emphasis in place with an 80 ms fade. Hot-seat camera changes are review tooling; production remote-seat views must keep a stable orientation.

**Sound cues.** Play ready once when decision priority reaches the viewing player, −18 dB, 120–180 ms. No opponent-ready cue reveals response availability or hand content. Plain Pass can use one low-priority confirm cue; do not stack ready/confirm repeatedly during automatic transitions. Deduplicate by accepted transition and seat. Mandatory-choice cues override routine ready cues in the shared mix. Mute preserves the same visible status and focused control.

**Acceptance.** Trace play → opponent response → pass → actor response → pass → native resolution, then obstruction, both buffs and both before-damage priorities. At each state compare actor and waiting-seat actions; test repeat/stale submissions, keyboard-held input, response cancellation and a mandatory choice during a response window. Native actor/status tests and production combat timing samples exist. Current observed failures include a missing actor in the pass sentence and hot-seat orientation changes. Remaining per-seat audiovisual and viewport acceptance is listed in validation; working native priority is not full UX approval.

**Public pacing repair (2026-10-10).** The adapter now adds public `decisionSeat` to its game-owned viewer projection using its existing native `getActivePlayerId`. It exposes only the seat, not private targets/candidates. Practice keeps the typed adapter projection; the live parser preserves this field and the existing `responseWindow.openFor/passedPlayers` as public priority/pass history. The live parser previously dropped that response data. HUD shows decision separately; it uses native decision seat for the step owner when no response window exists. YOUR CHOICE takes precedence over generic response copy while the viewer owes a required target. No generic gameplay protocol or engine-rule change is made.

## ac-turn — End turn and automatic events

**Intent and context.** The active player finishes Primary and yields to native end/start/Expansion events. Use `arena` for ordinary advancement, `optional-void` for a delayed zone change, and `prompt-foretell-draw-step` for a choice that interrupts drawing. Only a native advertised End turn action may begin the transition. Drawn card identity is private to its owner.

**Visual sequence.** Available: show End turn beside the current phase, separated from card actions. Hover/focus: emphasize that control without dimming the board. Selected: a deliberate button activation creates no speculative draw or ready change. Pending: hold the old board until acceptance and disable duplicate end-turn input. Resolving: show native end events, then the next seat marker, ready batch, draw and any mandatory prompt in causal order. Completed: expose Resource Step controls or the next actual native decision; never jump over a triggered choice. [Annotated layout](storyboards.html#turn).

**Input behavior.** Click or keyboard activation ends the turn once. Do not bind an unmodified global Space key to End turn while card inspection or a choice has focus. An unresolved native choice suppresses End turn. After acceptance, only engine-supported undo is available; cancelling decorative motion cannot restore a turn. Rejected/stale activation leaves the board unchanged and refreshes the action control. New prompts receive focus; inspection remains available during noninteractive animation.

**Board communication.** Use a compact phase track and a clear decision-seat marker. Ready/spent changes use orientation plus a state mark. Show a back travelling from deck to the opponent's hand; never display its face or name. Use public draw counts only. Batch simultaneous ready changes while preserving individual final states. A delayed card leaving for Oblivion must show its source and destination before the next choice. If drawing ends the match, stop ordinary phase feedback and use the terminal specification.

**Motion timeline.** After accepted state, phase emphasis changes over 220 ms. Ready cards rotate together over 180 ms; a draw uses Transfer 320 ms with at most 40 ms stagger for a multi-card native draw. Respect event causality rather than adding a fixed wait between every event. Keep each simultaneous batch within the shared 700 ms visual budget; expose a new legal decision immediately. Cards travel above the board and below choice panels. Reset cancels all old transition tails. Reduced motion uses immediate positions, static state marks and an 80 ms fade, with no camera sweep.

**Sound cues.** One confirm for accepted End turn, one ready cue for the entire ready batch, and one place cue at draw landing. The owner and opponent receive identity-neutral draw sounds. Multiple native draws share the two-voice place cap and cooldown; no per-card burst at high counts. A mandatory choice or terminal result takes priority over routine phase cues. Master mute stops active voices and prevents queued tails; no phase information depends on audio.

**Acceptance.** Verify ordinary end/start, the first-turn draw exception through native opening, engaged-card ready state, private drawn identity, delayed Void, Foretell interruption and failed draw. Check accepted turn counter and decision actor rather than animation completion. Repeat with both seats, keyboard, stale submission, reduced motion and mute. Existing native Foretell/finish tests and browser samples cover parts of this sequence; a combined full audiovisual sequence and all requested layouts remain open. Full historical provenance of arranged midgame boards is still required.

**Variants/status.** `arena`, `optional-void`, `prompt-foretell-draw-step`, `finish-deckout`; native support, partial browser evidence, target UX not yet accepted.

Resolving: finish the authoritative damage or failed-draw cue, then show the result. Completed: identify winner and cause in plain language (for example, “Player 1 wins — opponent could not draw”). When a fixture reviewer changes perspective, winner text and result color must use the same displayed seat as the board, including after the handoff veil. After native completion, expose inspection, history and local Reset; no gameplay actions. Dragging cannot alter the terminal board. Keyboard focus moves to the result heading once, then allows inspection and reset.

**Board communication.** Preserve the lethal source-to-target relationship until the impact finishes. For failed draw, highlight the empty deck and then the affected Contender; do not invent a card flight. Use a result icon plus winner text, never color or audio alone. Reduced motion uses a single 80 ms fade with no zoom, shake or board movement. These are design targets, not measured current timings.

**Sound.** Use a short neutral result cue for failed draw/concession and a restrained win/loss variation after lethal impact. Asset brief: soft low impact plus short tonal resolve, no speech or looping fanfare. Trigger once on terminal state, at result-frame onset; result priority exceeds routine pick/hover sounds, with one result voice and no replay on rerender. Duck an overlapping damage tail by 6 dB instead of stacking a second impact. Mute produces no result audio; all winner/cause information remains visible. Current result audio and overlap quality still require recordings.

**Acceptance.** Native tests prove both seats report game-over and expose no actions for lethal and failed draw. Browser screenshots prove the native result and public log for each. Concession cancellation/confirmation has native atomicity proof. Check reset, repeated renders, both seat perspectives, keyboard inspection, muted/reduced-motion result sequences and all three viewports before accepting UX. Evidence: `native-interaction-coverage.test.ts`, [failed draw](evidence/finish-deckout-1366.jpg), [lethal](evidence/finish-lethal-1366.jpg). ## ac-cost-routes — Normal, alternate and additional costs

### 1. Intent and context

Let the acting player compare legal ways to pay before committing. `prompt-cost-routes` starts in Player 1's turn-13 Primary with Shadowlight, Darkness Rising in a three-card hand, a white Shadowlight in Clash Zone, and six ready resources. The native alternative returns that friendly white Shadowlight to hand instead of paying two resources. `prompt-alternate-cost` preserves the existing related route; inspect its source-specific costs rather than assuming identical candidates. `prompt-additional-cost` requires Chaotic Unmaking's extra discard. `prompt-discount-cost` begins before Player 2's Sonoro attack, so Player 1 must reach the actual Counter window before choosing Enter from Beyond's normal or discard-discount route.

Only the actor sees private hand payment candidates. The opponent sees public costs and zone/count changes, not a private route draft. Rules and eligible cards come from the adapter; the shared surface renders route and destination data.

### 2. Visual sequence

[Six annotated states](storyboards.html#cost-routes) are proposed acceptance views, not screenshots of implemented behavior.

- **Available:** source in hand or legal source zone, with a play/response affordance. Inspection names each legal action.
- **Hover/focus:** source outline and a short cost summary. Do not highlight a preferred strategic route.
