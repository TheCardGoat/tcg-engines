# Judge Calls and Appeals

Any role participating in a tournament may call for a judge with a question. In between rounds, tournament participants and personnel can approach judges for questions. This should be done in a calm and orderly manner, particularly if multiple people require a judge’s assistance. During games in a round, players should remain seated and may verbally call out for a judge or physically motion or signal to a judge for assistance. Simply requesting a judge suffices; players do not need to yell the reason for the judge call or be disruptive to gather a judge's attention. Judges should be attentive to any player seeking a judge's attention and should attempt to respond in the order in which the calls are made. Judges should also be attentive to player interactions and be proactive in responding to situations that might result in or should involve a judge call, even if a call is not explicitly made. All calls for a judge from tournament participants or personnel should be done calmly and respectfully. Spectators may discreetly request that a judge view a particular game or match. Any player or spectator may interrupt a game to call for a judge if:

* A player threatens any tournament attendees
* There is an issue or emergency that requires a tournament official
* A player needs to leave or has left the table for any reason.

In tournaments where there are both appointed Head and Floor judges are present, Floor Judges should be the first ones to respond to a judge call. Players cannot request a specific or different judge to attend a judge call and this applies to both the initial call and any appeals in which a Head Judge is involved. **Before any penalties are applied** from a decision made by the attending judge, the ruling may be appealed.

A brief breakdown for the typical process in which a player-initiated judge call begins and finishes is as follows:

1. The player signals (verbally, physically) for a judge, requesting their presence to a match.
2. The player calling the judge briefly explains the context and reason for the call. Please refer to the section on [Player and Judge Calls](/player-communication/judge-call-ettiquette.md) for more advice on judge call etiquette.
3. The attending judge will ask further clarification questions about the call to gather more information. If needed, the judge will interview each player separately and/or in private (if needed), as well as any spectators or nearby players.
4. The judge will deliver a final outcome to the judge call, whether it is answering a generic question (e.g., bathroom break), an interaction question or clarification, or an infraction that is being penalized.
   1. If a violation has occurred, the judge is expected to name the infraction, state the penalty, and explain the intended corrective procedure.
5. In the case that a judge is delivering a ruling on an infraction, the player has the right to **appeal** the call **before** any corrective procedures and penalties are applied.

{% hint style="warning" %}
After corrective procedures have been applied, they sometimes can't be undone. Appeals should be handled before these procedures so they only happen once!
{% endhint %}

#### Appealing a Call

When a call attended by floor or regular judge is appealed, the next step will be to bring their supervising Assistant Head Judge to the call. There, the Assistant Head Judge will introduce themselves and will ask any other investigational questions they feel are warranted. It is recommended they discuss the context of the call with the attending judge(s) and discuss the intended ruling and corrective action with them before delivering it to the player(s) in the match. After this, the player may still ask for the ruling to be appealed or escalated once more to the Head Judge.

When the ruling is escalated to the Head Judge, the Assistant Head Judge and Floor Judge can present the information about the call. The Head Judge should introduce themselves before beginning their own investigation to clarify or ask any other questions. **Any ruling issued by a Head Judge, whether on an initial call or after an appeal, is&#x20;*****final.*** Any ruling and procedures applied by the Head Judge can't be appealed any further in the context of the tournament.

Calls will typically be attended by floor or regular judges initially while the presence of an Assistant Head Judge and Head Judge are reserved for any escalations or appeals. The Assistant Head Judge should be present any time a game loss is issued to confirm the penalty while the Head Judge must be present for any match loss or higher penalty. The Head Judge should discuss and report and disqualifications with the Tournament Organizer.

{% hint style="success" %}
Serious violations which might result in match losses or disqualifications are sometimes discussed between the Head Judge and the Tournament Organizer before a final judgement is delivered.
{% endhint %}

Players are welcome to offer an appeal or escalate a ruling by submitting an email ticket to Weebs if they are acting in good faith and have good cause to believe a final ruling was made in error, was unreasonable, or a gross departure from the philosophies regarding tournament integrity we promote and enforce at our events.
